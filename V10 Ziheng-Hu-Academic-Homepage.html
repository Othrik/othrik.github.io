About

Where computation meets the bench.

An undergraduate trying to build familiarity on both sides of modern pharmaceutical science — dry-lab simulation and wet-lab experimentation.

I'm an undergraduate student in pharmaceutical sciences at Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University (XJTLU). My interests sit at the intersection of computational drug design and protein engineering, though I'm still early in my journey and learning on both sides.

In the Wu Lab, I worked on small-molecule inhibitors targeting myostatin (MSTN) — running virtual screening, molecular docking, and binding free energy analysis. This work led to a co-first author manuscript submitted to eLife.

As a Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship (SURF) researcher with the XPad Team, I'm learning to engineer affibody-based protein therapeutics through the wet-lab pipeline — cloning, expression, purification, and binding characterization. Being able to move between computation and experiment is what I find most interesting, even if I still have a lot to learn.

Research Interests

Four directions, one bridge.

The questions I find most interesting sit at the boundary between computation and experiment. These are the directions I'm currently exploring.

01

Protein Therapeutics Design

Engineered binding proteins (affibody/nanobody), from computational design toward wet-lab testing — still learning the full cycle.

02

Computational Drug Design

Molecular dynamics, free energy calculations, virtual screening — learning to use these tools to ask better questions.

03

Bridging Computation & Experiment

Moving between in silico predictions and wet-lab results — the part of research I find most rewarding.

04

Allosteric Regulation & Dynamics

How conformational dynamics shape protein function, and whether transient states might be worth targeting — a direction I'm beginning to explore.
